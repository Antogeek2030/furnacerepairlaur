When a furnace fails, the first question is usually repair or replace. Here is a straightforward framework we use with Laurel homeowners.

## Age of the Unit

- **Under 10–12 years:** Repair is usually the better value if the issue is isolated.
- **12–18 years:** Evaluate carefully. A major component failure may tip the scale toward replacement.
- **Over 18 years:** Replacement often makes more sense, especially if efficiency is low or repairs are recurring.

## Cost of the Repair

A common rule of thumb: if the repair approaches 50% of the cost of a new system, and the unit is older, replacement deserves serious consideration. We always provide a written estimate so you can compare options clearly.

## Efficiency and Comfort

Older furnaces (especially those under 80% AFUE) cost more to operate. Newer high-efficiency models can reduce heating bills and improve comfort with better temperature control and quieter operation.

## Safety Considerations

A cracked heat exchanger or repeated carbon monoxide concerns are strong reasons to replace rather than repair. Safety always comes first.
