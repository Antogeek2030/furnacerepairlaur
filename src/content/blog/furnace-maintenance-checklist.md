A few minutes of attention each month can prevent many mid-winter furnace failures. Use this checklist to keep your system in better shape between professional visits.

## Monthly Checks

- **Replace or clean the air filter.** A clogged filter is the leading cause of restricted airflow and short cycling.
- **Listen for new sounds.** Note any new rattles, squeals, or bangs and schedule service if they persist.
- **Check vents and returns.** Make sure furniture, curtains, or rugs are not blocking airflow.

## Before Heating Season

- Schedule a professional tune-up. Technicians inspect the heat exchanger, ignition system, safety controls, and combustion (on gas units).
- Test the thermostat. Set it a few degrees above room temperature and confirm the furnace starts and runs normally.
- Clear the area around the furnace. Keep storage, boxes, and flammable materials away from the unit.

## When to Call a Pro Immediately

- No heat at all
- Gas smell (evacuate and call the gas company first)
- Carbon monoxide detector activation
- Water leaking near the furnace (on high-efficiency units with condensate)
- Persistent burning or electrical smells

Regular professional maintenance remains the best insurance against emergency repairs. Laurel-area homeowners who schedule annual tune-ups typically see fewer mid-season failures and better efficiency.
