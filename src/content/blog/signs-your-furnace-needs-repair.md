Maryland winters arrive fast. Waiting until the first freezing night to discover your furnace has a problem is a stressful way to start the season. Here are five signs you should schedule a diagnosis now.

## 1. Uneven Rooms or Weak Airflow

If some rooms are warm while others stay chilly, or if air from the vents feels weak, the blower, ducts, or filter may be restricting flow. A simple filter change sometimes helps — but persistent issues need professional attention.

## 2. Strange Noises

Banging, squealing, rattling, or whistling are not normal. Delayed ignition, worn bearings, and loose parts all produce distinctive sounds. Addressing noise early often prevents a full breakdown.

## 3. Short Cycling

A furnace that turns on and off every few minutes is short cycling. This wastes energy and stresses components. Common causes include dirty filters, oversized equipment, or a failing limit switch.

## 4. Rising Energy Bills

If your heating costs jump without a change in usage or rates, the furnace may be working harder than it should. A tune-up or targeted repair often restores efficiency.

## 5. Yellow Pilot Flame or Frequent Pilot Outages

On older units, a yellow (instead of blue) pilot flame or repeated outages can indicate incomplete combustion or a failing thermocouple. These issues can also create safety concerns.

## What to Do Next

If you notice any of these signs in your Laurel home, schedule a diagnostic visit before the peak heating season. Same-day appointments are often available, and most first-visit repairs restore reliable heat quickly.
